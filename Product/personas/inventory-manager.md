### Pains
- **Expired stock goes undetected until physical checks.** Without batch tracking or expiry alerts, expired drugs and vaccines sit on shelves until Mohan does a manual audit (roughly every 2-4 weeks). Expired items are pure financial loss, and dispensing expired drugs is a safety risk he worries about
- **Stockouts during consultations disrupt patient care.** The vet discovers a critical drug is out of stock mid-consultation. Mohan gets called away from whatever he is doing to check, confirm the stockout, and either rush to a nearby pharmacy or call a distributor for emergency delivery. This happens 2-3 times per week
- **Want-list creation is entirely manual.** To prepare a reorder list, Mohan walks through the stockroom, opens every cabinet, counts items by hand, and types quantities into a WhatsApp message to each distributor. The process takes 30-45 minutes and is error-prone -- he frequently forgets items or miscounts
- **No FIFO enforcement for dispensing.** Newer stock gets placed in front, and older stock gets pushed to the back. Without a system enforcing first-in-first-out, older batches expire while newer ones are consumed first
- **Multi-role demands fragment his attention.** Because he also assists in surgery and covers the front desk, he cannot dedicate focused blocks to inventory management. Stock checks happen in 10-minute bursts between other duties, leading to incomplete counts and missed items

### What is This Person Trying to Accomplish?
- Know which items are below reorder level without physically checking every shelf
- Generate a want-list automatically and share it with distributors via WhatsApp in one tap
- Track expiry dates for every batch and get alerts before items expire
- Scan barcodes to record stock receipts and dispense items instead of manual register entries
- Account for every unit of stock movement (received, dispensed, returned, damaged) with a clear audit trail

### Goals
- **Short-term:** Eliminate the weekly 45-minute manual stock count by having real-time inventory levels on his phone
- **Long-term:** Run a zero-stockout, zero-expired-waste stockroom where every item is tracked from receipt to dispensing with batch-level precision
- **Personal:** Be recognized by the clinic owner as someone who saves money through better stock management, which could justify a raise or a more formal role

### Attitudes & Influences
- **Decision-Making Authority:** No -- the clinic owner (vet) decides on software purchases. Mohan influences the decision by reporting problems (stockouts, expired items, order delays) that create visible pain for the vet. If Mohan says "this system helps me track stock better," the vet is likely to approve it
- **Decision Influencers:** Distributor representatives who suggest ordering tools, other pharmacy-background staff he knows from his previous job, and the clinic vet's frustration with stockouts. He trusts tools he can see working immediately -- a barcode scan that pulls up item details is more convincing than a dashboard full of charts
- **Beliefs & Attitudes:** Practical and hands-on; prefers physical verification over digital claims ("I still count by hand to be sure"). Will adopt digital tools if they are faster than his current method, but will not use them if they add steps to his workflow. Comfortable with barcode scanners from his pharmacy background. Wary of tools that require internet connectivity because the stockroom has weak Wi-Fi signal. Wants something that works in Hindi or is simple enough that language does not matter

### Breeyo Touchpoints
- **Features used most:** Inventory item list with stock levels (Phase 5), barcode scanning for receipt and dispensing (Phase 5), batch/lot tracking with expiry management (Phase 5), par-level alerts and want-list generation (Phase 5), stock-take mode (Phase 5), WhatsApp share for want-lists (Phase 5/7)
- **Primary workflow:** Morning: opens inventory screen -> checks attention card for items below par level or nearing expiry -> addresses urgent items. During the day: receives stock delivery -> scans barcodes -> records batch number and expiry -> shelves items. When vet dispenses a drug during consultation: stock is auto-decremented. Weekly: runs stock-take using scan-and-count mode -> reviews discrepancies -> adjusts
- **What success looks like:** He gets a push notification saying "Amoxicillin 250mg: 4 strips remaining, below par level of 10" before the vet ever asks for it. He taps "Generate Want-List" and a formatted message with all below-par items goes to his distributor on WhatsApp. He never finds expired stock on the shelf because the system warned him 30 days before expiry
